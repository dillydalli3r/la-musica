# la musica 4.1.2 — the phone's playback, made observable

4.1.2 answers the owner's report that iOS playback *still* does not work —
"audio stops and the controls don't work", with the OS card showing no app
identity — and it answers it with evidence rather than another guess. One
mechanism changed, and one thing that was invisible is now a screen.

## The keep-alive was rendering silence, which may not count

4.1.1 gave the app process a keep-alive so iOS would not suspend it while the
webview's music played: a looping `AVAudioPlayer` on the app's own playback
session. It played **digital silence** — and digital silence is the one signal a
platform can discount as "no audio", which would leave the process exactly as
suspendable as before the buffer existed (the report arrived the same day).

The buffer is now **dither**: ±1 LSB of a deterministic generator, about
−90 dBFS, generated by `keepalive_wav`. Inaudible under any master, and a real
signal (measured with `volumedetect`: max = mean = −90.3 dB). Everything else
about the keep-alive is unchanged — it runs only while the web player is playing
AND the app is in the background, and `MPNowPlayingInfoCenter` is still left
alone.

## What the shell is doing is now readable from the app

The two reports cost a release each because they describe a phone, and no
development box can observe one. So the shell publishes its own state —
`ios_audio_state`, a flat key/value list — and the app draws it:

* **Settings → Downloads & playback → Playback diagnostics** shows the shell's
  iOS audio state: the session's **category as the framework's own getter
  reports it** (so "did `configure` run" is a fact, not an assumption), whether
  other audio is playing, the output volume, whether the **app believes it is in
  the background**, whether the **web player says it is playing**, and whether a
  **keep-alive is actually rendering** — with the reason when it is not.
* Beside it, the web player's own black box (`web/src/lib/pbDiag.ts`, cap 40):
  the element's `play`/`pause`/`ended`/`error`/`stalled`/`waiting` events **with
  the visibility state and readyState at that moment**, every Media Session
  action the OS actually sent (so "the lock-screen button did nothing" becomes
  "the OS never told the page"), every seek with its source, the shared
  `AudioContext`'s state transitions, and a **heartbeat gap** event when a tick
  arrives 3 s or more late.
* Two **heartbeats** sit beside those rows, because "which process froze" is the
  question the reports cannot answer from the outside: the page's own 1 s tick
  (a gap of 3 s or more means the web content process was frozen or suspended)
  and the shell's (`app_process_worst_gap_s` — a multi-second gap there means
  iOS suspended the APP while the webview's process survived).
* Copy puts the whole report on the clipboard, so a screenshot is not the only
  way to hand it over.

Those signatures are distinguishable, and that is the point: a large heartbeat
gap at the moment the audio stopped means the web content process was frozen; a
small gap with the element's own `pause` means the platform paused the element
in a live process; a heartbeat that keeps ticking while the audio stops means the
app process was suspended while the webview's survived.

## Why the OS card looks like web media

A WKWebView's audio is published to iOS by **WebKit's web content process** —
`MediaSessionManagerCocoa::sessionWillBeginPlayback` hands the now-playing info
to MediaRemote from that process, and
`RemoteCommandListenerCocoa::updateSupportedCommands` publishes its own
(transport-only) command set from the same origin. The shell's own registrations
— the star is one — belong to the app's process. That is the architectural reason
the card carries no app identity and offers the platform's web-media controls:
while the audio lives in the webview, the webview *is* the player. Making iOS
treat la musica as the media app means playing the audio in the app, natively,
which is a change of its own (and one that can keep the equalizer, ReplayGain and
the visualizer by moving them to `AVAudioEngine` — but not in this release).
